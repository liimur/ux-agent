# Sketches and prototypes

These are operational interpretations, not fixed laws. Apply them to the decision at hand, resources and risk. Source: Bill Buxton, *Sketching User Experiences* (2007), “The Anatomy of Sketching,” pp. 111–112, and “Sketches are not Prototypes,” pp. 138–140.

- **PR-1 — State the decision.** Before choosing a medium or fidelity, write what the artifact is meant to explore or evaluate and which aspects are intentionally unresolved.
- **PR-2 — Explore alternatives cheaply.** When direction is uncertain, develop distinct, inexpensive concepts that can be discarded or combined. Compare their consequences against the task and constraints rather than polishing the first option by default.
- **PR-3 — Signal uncertainty.** Make an exploratory sketch visibly provisional and include only detail needed for the question. Avoid visual polish that implies undecided content or behavior is final.
- **PR-4 — Match the artifact to its purpose.** Use sketches to generate and discuss possibilities; make a prototype sufficiently concrete to investigate the particular behavior or experience being evaluated. This is a continuum, not a rigid fidelity ladder: a rough prototype can also support early testing.
- **PR-5 — Evaluate with appropriate evidence.** As investment and consequences increase, make selection criteria explicit and seek relevant user feedback. Iterate: feedback can reopen exploration rather than merely validate a chosen direction.
- **PR-6 — Describe coverage.** Record the intended scenario, supported interactions, simulated parts and untested states. Do not claim an artifact validates an experience it does not represent.

**Limits:** A sketch is not evidence of usability. The book's distinction between sketches and prototypes is a useful framing, not a requirement to delay user feedback or adopt a particular tool.

## Paper simulation

Source: Carolyn Snyder, *Paper Prototyping* (2003), ch. 4, “Simulating Interaction.” The available copy was flagged as a possible excerpt; this rule is limited to the section inspected.

- **PR-7 — Make simulation limits visible.** When paper or a person simulates interface feedback, explain the test convention to participants, record what was simulated, and avoid inferring precise timing or subtle interaction behavior that the simulation cannot faithfully represent. Follow up with a more representative prototype when those qualities matter.
